# Spec — Vendor Pricing Comparison (worked example)

A complete, realistic build contract. Copy this shape for your own projects; the point is the level of specificity, not the subject.

**Intent:** A product team is deciding how to price a new tier and keeps arguing from half-remembered competitor numbers. They need a single page showing how comparable vendors actually structure pricing, with every figure traceable to the vendor's own page, so the pricing debate argues about strategy instead of about whose recollection is right.

**Goals**

- Compare pricing *structure* (per-seat, usage-metered, hybrid, credit-based) across five named competitors, not just headline prices — structure is the decision-relevant variable.
- Show each figure with a visible citation and the date it was verified.
- Mark anything not officially published as unknown rather than estimating it.

**Non-goals**

- No recommendation on what to charge. This is evidence for a decision, not the decision.
- No forecast of competitor pricing moves.
- No analyst-report figures; vendor pages only, so every number is checkable by the reader.

**Audience:** The product and finance leads making the pricing call, plus anyone who later asks "where did this number come from?" six months on.

**Tier:** T1 — internal, reputation-bearing, but nobody's system depends on it.

**Build mode:** one-shot.

**Notes for Scout:** Commercial figures require tier 0 sources — the vendor's own pricing page or filings. Where a vendor publishes no price for an enterprise tier, record it in `unknowns`; do not carry a third-party estimate into the factsheet. Report conflicting figures as a spread with both citations.
